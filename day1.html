Section 1 · Staying Safe Online

Passwords, phishing, privacy, and everyday habits that keep you protected.

Why this matters

Almost everything you do — banking, school, chatting with friends, shopping — now happens through a screen. That convenience comes with risk: scammers, data thieves, and malicious software are all designed to look ordinary. The good news is that a handful of habits stop the vast majority of attacks.

1. Passwords & two-factor authentication

A weak or reused password is the single easiest way for someone to break into your accounts. Best practice:

  • Use a different password for every important account.
  • Make passwords long (12+ characters) rather than clever — a random passphrase like correct-horse-battery-lamp beats P@ssw0rd!.
  • Use a password manager to generate and remember them for you.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever it's offered — it adds a second check (like a code on your phone) so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
Common mistake Writing "Fluffy2010!" as your password because it's "personal and hard to guess." Anything findable on your social media (pet names, birthdays, schools) is one of the first things attackers try.

2. Spotting phishing & scams

Phishing is when someone impersonates a trustworthy source (your bank, a delivery company, even a friend) to trick you into clicking a link, downloading a file, or handing over information. Look for these red flags:

⏱️ Urgency & pressure

"Act now or your account will be suspended." Real organizations rarely demand instant action.

🔗 Mismatched links

Hover over a link before clicking — if the text says "yourbank.com" but the actual URL is something odd, don't click.

✉️ Odd sender address

"support@amaz0n-security.net" is not Amazon. Check the full email address, not just the display name.

🎁 Too good to be true

Unexpected prizes, refunds, or job offers that ask you to pay or "verify" info first are almost always scams.

3. Protecting your privacy

  • Review app and social media privacy settings — limit who can see your posts, location, and contact info.
  • Think before you post: photos, locations, and personal details can be seen far beyond your intended audience.
  • Be cautious on public Wi-Fi — avoid banking or entering passwords on open networks; a VPN adds a layer of protection.
  • Check the padlock (HTTPS) before entering sensitive info — but remember, it only means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest.

Do

  • Use a password manager + 2FA
  • Verify requests through a separate channel (call the company directly)
  • Keep software and apps updated
  • Pause before clicking anything urgent

Don't

  • Reuse passwords across sites
  • Click links in unexpected messages
  • Share verification codes with anyone
  • Assume HTTPS means "safe and honest"

Section 2 · Mastering Google Search

Search operators, filters, and habits that get you to the right page fast.

Think in keywords, not sentences

Google doesn't need a full question — it needs the words that matter. Instead of typing "what is the best way to remove a coffee stain from a white shirt", try remove coffee stain white shirt. Fewer, sharper words usually beat a long sentence.

Search operators — your shortcuts

These small symbols and words dramatically narrow your results:

OperatorWhat it doesExample
"..."Match the exact phrase, word for word"internet safety course"
-wordExclude a word from results (no space after the minus)jaguar -car
site:Search only one website or domainsite:wikipedia.org climate
filetype:Find a specific file typerenewable energy filetype:pdf
ORMatch either term (capital letters required)python OR javascript course
*Wildcard — fill in the blank"the * of the internet"
intitle:Word must appear in the page titleintitle:phishing guide
define:Quick dictionary definitiondefine:credible
Worked example Looking for a downloadable government report on online scams? Try: online scams report filetype:pdf site:.gov. That combines a file-type filter with a domain filter to skip blogs and ads entirely.

Use Google's built-in filters

🕒 Tools → Time range

Click "Tools" under the search bar to filter results to the past hour, day, week, or a custom date range — great for current events.

🖼️ Images / News / Books tabs

Switch tabs above the results to search specifically within images, news articles, books, or maps.

🔍 Reverse image search

Search by uploading or pasting an image URL to find its source, similar images, or where else it's been used — handy for checking if a photo is real or recycled.

🧮 Quick answers

Google can calculate, convert units, and give word definitions directly in the search box — no extra site needed.

Do

  • Start broad, then add operators to narrow down
  • Use quotes for exact phrases and names
  • Check the Tools menu for date filters
  • Scroll past ads (labeled "Sponsored") to organic results

Don't

  • Type full questions when a few keywords will do
  • Trust the very first result automatically
  • Ignore the URL/domain before clicking
  • Forget that ranking ≠ reliability
