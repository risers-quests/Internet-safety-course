Why this matters

You do so many things on a screen — games, schoolwork, chatting with friends. That's great! But some people online try to trick others. The good news: a few simple habits keep you safe.

1. Passwords keep your stuff locked

A weak password is like leaving your front door open. Here's how to lock it tight:

  • Use a different password for every account.
  • Make it long, like a silly sentence: purple-dragon-eats-tacos is much stronger than Pass123.
  • A password manager is an app that remembers your passwords for you.
  • Two-factor authentication (2FA) means a second lock — like a code texted to your phone — so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
Common mistake Using "Fluffy2010!" because it feels personal. If it's on your social media (a pet's name, a birthday), it's one of the first things a trickster will try.

2. Spotting tricky messages (phishing)

Phishing is when someone pretends to be someone you trust — like a store or even a friend — to get you to click a link or share information. Watch for these clues:

⏱️ It rushes you

"Click now or lose your account!" Real companies almost never demand instant action.

🔗 The link looks odd

Look closely before you click — if it says one thing but the web address looks strange, don't click.

✉️ The sender looks fake

"amaz0n-security.net" is not Amazon. Check the whole address, not just the name shown.

🎁 It's too good to be true

A surprise prize that asks you to pay first or "verify" info first is almost always a trick.

3. Keeping your information private

  • Check your privacy settings — decide who can see your posts and your location.
  • Think before you post — pictures and details can travel further than you expect.
  • Be careful on public Wi-Fi — don't type passwords or bank details on it.
  • A padlock and "https" mean the connection is locked — but it doesn't mean the site is honest.

Do

  • Use a password manager + 2FA
  • Ask a trusted adult if a message feels weird
  • Keep apps updated
  • Pause before clicking anything urgent

Don't

  • Reuse passwords everywhere
  • Click links in surprise messages
  • Share codes or passwords with anyone
  • Assume a padlock means "totally safe"

Use a few words, not a whole sentence

Google doesn't need a full question. Instead of typing "what is the best way to remove a coffee stain from a white shirt", just try remove coffee stain white shirt. Fewer words, better results.

Search tricks — your shortcuts

These small symbols help Google understand exactly what you want:

TrickWhat it doesExample
"..."Find those exact words, in that exact order"internet safety course"
-wordLeave a word out of your resultsjaguar -car
site:Search inside just one websitesite:kids.nationalgeographic.com sharks
filetype:Find a certain kind of filerenewable energy filetype:pdf
ORFind either word (capital letters needed)python OR javascript course
*A stand-in for a missing word"the * of the internet"
define:A quick definitiondefine:credible
Try it yourself Looking for a downloadable report about online scams? Try: online scams report filetype:pdf site:.gov. That skips blogs and ads and goes straight to official PDFs.

Use Google's built-in filters

🕒 Tools → Time range

Click "Tools" under the search bar to see only recent results — great for news and current events.

🖼️ Images / News / Books tabs

Switch tabs above your results to search just pictures, news, books, or maps.

🔍 Reverse image search

Upload a picture to find where else it's been used — a great way to check if a photo is real.

🧮 Quick answers

Google can do math, convert units, and give quick definitions right in the search box.

Do

  • Start with a few words, then narrow down
  • Use quotes for exact names or phrases
  • Check the Tools menu for date filters
  • Skip past ads marked "Sponsored"

Don't

  • Type a whole question when a few words work
  • Trust the very first result without checking
  • Ignore the web address before clicking
  • Assume "first" means "best"
